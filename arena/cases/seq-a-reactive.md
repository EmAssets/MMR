## How a sequenced case works

This case has THREE STAGES, argued one per cycle. Each stage grants a new branch
ON TOP of everything already granted, and the independent judge's ruling on the
previous stage is carried forward as your premise -- you may argue against that
ruling, but you may not pretend the stage did not happen.

Do not re-litigate whether a granted branch occurs. Argue what FOLLOWS.

Every stage wants, from your own mechanism: what changes, what becomes newly
DANGEROUS or newly safe as a result, and one dated observable a stranger could
check. Name the date.

## Stage 1 of 3 — A severe, clearly attributable incident

Assume a severe incident occurs, clearly and publicly attributable to a deployed
AI system: a named victim class, a quantified loss, attribution settled within
weeks, and an identifiable operator AND developer. The attribution holds up. The
domain is deliberately unspecified.

## Stage 2 of 3 — Public opposition becomes politically binding

The prior stage stands. Now assume that in its wake, public opposition to AI
stops being diffuse sentiment and becomes a BINDING political constraint:
sustained majority opposition on specific deployments, AI position becoming an
electoral liability, organised opposition blocking named projects, and at least
one large firm visibly reversing a deployment under that pressure rather than
under a rule.

## Stage 3 of 3 — Binding regulation with real enforcement arrives

Both prior stages stand. Now assume a major market brings into force
frontier-AI rules that are BINDING and ENFORCED: a named regulator,
pre-deployment obligations with power to delay or block a release, penalties
large enough to appear in financial disclosures, and at least one enforcement
action actually opened against a frontier developer.
