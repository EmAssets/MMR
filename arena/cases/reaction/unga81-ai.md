# UNGA 81 — AI governance at the General Debate
as of: 2026-09-22

## Chronology — dated, sourced facts only

- **2024** — The Global Digital Compact, adopted at the Summit of the Future,
  commits to creating a Global Dialogue on AI Governance.
- **A/RES/79/325** — UN General Assembly resolution formally establishes the
  Global Dialogue on AI Governance. Co-chairs: Egriselda López (El Salvador) and
  Rein Tammsaar (Estonia).
- **2026-07-06** — The inaugural session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance
  is held in Geneva. The Secretary-General delivers opening remarks.
- **2026-09-22** — Khalilur Rahman, President of the 81st session of the General
  Assembly, opens the general debate. On AI he says it "can be a powerful
  accelerator of human progress; therefore, we must not close the door to
  innovation," names "job losses and widening of the digital divide" as harms,
  and says "artificial general intelligence, or AGI, could affect the very human
  survival." He calls for "prudent and reliable guardrails, based on scientific
  evidence." He names no mechanism, panel or deadline.
- **2026-09-22** — Donald Trump, US President, addresses the General Debate.
  He states: "The United States totally rejects any attempt to construct a
  globalist scheme to control artificial intelligence." He proposes renaming the
  technology: "we will officially call it superintelligence, changing the name,"
  rejecting the word "artificial." He dismisses regulatory concerns regarding
  cybersecurity, the labour market and the environment. He proposes no
  regulatory framework.
- **2026-09-22** — Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, President of Turkey, addresses the
  General Debate. He states: "It is long overdue now to establish a common
  international legal framework for AI," and calls to "accelerate the work... to
  draft and bring into force the UN international convention on AI." He cites
  disinformation and cybersecurity threats. He provides no treaty text.
- **2026-09-22** — King Abdullah II of Jordan addresses the General Debate. He
  discusses technology's effect on attention spans, citing a fall from "2.5
  minutes in 2004 to just 47 seconds today." He does not address AI regulation
  or superintelligence.
- **2027-05-03** — The second session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance is
  scheduled for New York, back-to-back with the 11th Multi-stakeholder Forum on
  Science, Technology and Innovation.
