**Devil's advocate** — shared prior: All four lenses lean on the same small citation cluster — the NY governor's office press release/EO text, the Heatmap poll, Good Jobs First's tracker, and the law-firm client alerts (DLA Piper/Davis Polk/Paul Hastings) summarizing the same NY statute. Law-firm client alerts and advocacy trackers are optimized to make regulatory change look sweeping and fast (that's their business model — alerts drive billable engagements, trackers drive donor urgency), so convergence here is partly convergence on a shared marketing/advocacy framing, not four independent readings of primary legislative text. · counter-case: (1) Base rate for state legislative 'waves': hundreds of bills get introduced every session on every hot-button issue (see: 2021 crypto-mining moratoria, 2018-19 5G/cell-tower moratoria, 2023 short-term-rental bans) and the overwhelming majority die in committee or lapse — 'proposals' and even 'local ordinances' are cheap, reversible, and routinely superseded once a single high-profile economic-development deal is on the table; counting 100+ proposals as a cascade conflates bill introduction (near-zero cost, near-zero signal) with enactment (costly, rare). (2) NY's own law is explicitly temporary (one-year) and carves out DEC/executive-order authority rather than durable statute — that's the signature of a pressure-release valve designed to expire quietly once headlines move on, not a structural regime change; one-year moratoria are a classic legislative stalling tactic, not evidence of a sentiment-to-policy mechanism compressing. (3) Sanders-DeSantis alignment is being read as a powerful cross-partisan wedge, but the more mundane explanation is that both are opportunistically attaching to a local NIMBY fight that long predates AI salience — data-center/warehouse siting opposition (noise, water, tax abatements, land use) has been a live local issue since well before generative AI was a talking point, and re-labeling a land-use backlash as 'AI backlash' is a framing choice by the lenses, not a fact about voter motivation. (4) Selection effect: journalists and trackers (Heatmap, Good Jobs First, law firms) have strong incentives to cover the dramatic cases and ignore the much larger number of jurisdictions where data centers were approved without incident in the same nine months — the denominator (total siting decisions nationally) is never reported, so '100+ moratorium proposals' has no base rate to compare against and may just be normal land-use friction scaled to an industry that's building at record pace. · weakest convergence: The 'sentiment-to-legislation lag is compressing to weeks-to-months' claim (ai-public-backlash, echoed by ai-us-policy-direction's 'accelerating growth rate') is the weakest link. It rests on a single anecdote (NH governor moving ~1 month after a grid-operator price signal) generalized into a revised universal lag parameter, and it's the claim every other lens quietly imports without re-deriving — ai-compute-buildout calls the wave 'faster-moving than interconnect queues' and ai-us-policy-direction calls it 'accelerating' largely by citing the same NY timeline. This is a textbook single-case-to-general-rule extrapolation dressed up as a parameter revision, and it's exactly the kind of claim that four lenses reading the same three law-firm alerts would independently launder into 'confirmed, not just suspected.'
**Base-rate librarian** — class: State/local moratoria or bans on a specific, newly-controversial land-use/infrastructure type, enacted in response to a fast-rising, geographically diffuse public grievance (e.g., fracking bans/moratoria 2010-2014, cell tower/5G siting moratoria, LNG export terminal and pipeline local bans, big-box/Walmart siting bans 1990s-2000s, casino and landfill siting moratoria, short-term rental bans post-2015) (n: roughly 15-25 comparable multi-state infrastructure-siting backlash waves since 1990, each spawning dozens to hundreds of local/state proposals within 1-2 years of the grievance becoming salient; rate: in this class, the great majority of proposals never become enacted, binding law (most die in committee, get watered down to studies/setbacks, or are preempted/struck down); a minority (roughly 1-in-4 to 1-in-3) of jurisdictions that propose a moratorium actually enact one, and enacted moratoria are usually temporary, narrow, or later relaxed once the industry offers concessions (tax revenue sharing, price caps, remote siting) or the acute cost driver eases; full sector-wide multi-state bans that stick for years are rare (fracking bans in NY/VT persisted, but most fracking-state proposals didn't ban outright, and most cell-tower/5G moratoria were struck down or expired)) · outside view: Base rates say expect a fast proposal wave to keep growing in count for another 6-18 months, then plateau and fragment as industry buys off the sharpest grievance (electricity price relief, tax-sharing, remote siting) state by state; expect maybe 3-8 of the 100+ proposals to become real enacted law in the next year, not a cascade of 15+, and expect the coalition (Sanders-DeSantis) to fracture once concessions target each side's specific complaint, since cross-partisan alliances in this class are typically united by convenience rather than shared diagnosis and rarely survive contact with sector lobbying. · parts ways: The lenses treat the current velocity (100+ proposals in under a year, NY-to-law in ~6 weeks) as evidence the sentiment-to-law lag is structurally compressing for this issue; the outside view treats that velocity as normal early-wave enthusiasm that historically decelerates once industry concessions peel off the coalition, making the lenses' 15+ enactments and multi-state-law expectations likely overconfident on both count and durability.

Data centers are large, visible, unpopular new industrial neighbors (noise, water, land, grid strain) that make an easy local target regardless of AI hype — this is standard NIMBY-plus-utility-rate politics, the same pattern seen historically with power plants, landfills, and cell towers. Sanders and DeSantis agreeing on 'my constituents' bills went up, blame the nearby industrial facility' requires zero coordination — it's two populists independently reading the same polling. · where boring loses: If moratorium language explicitly targets AI/safety framing rather than electricity costs or land use in most enacted statutes (not just NY), or if the moratoriums keep spreading in states with flat/falling electricity prices, that would mean this is about AI specifically rather than ordinary utility-rate NIMBYism wearing an AI costume. · bet: 3:1 boring: this is ordinary rate-shock NIMBYism, not a novel backlash-mechanism or federal-preemption chess match — track electricity price data against moratorium adoption, not AI sentiment polls.
