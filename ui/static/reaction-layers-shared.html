Reaction structure · one event, 14 acts

Who was looking at what

On 9 September 2026 an Anthropic safety researcher resigned, forfeited his equity, and posted why. The post reached 172 million views. Then everyone reacted — but not to the same thing.

Sacks attacked “the proposal.” Altman and Musk agreed with “pacing the frontier.” Those are Dario Amodei’s words, from an essay published three days later. They were not responding to the resignation at all. Each ring below is one hop of reaction — and any of the fourteen can hold the centre. Stand with someone and the rings recount from where they stood.

The finding

Four of the loudest voices sit on ring 2, not ring 1. They were arguing about an audit regime that did not exist when the post went up.

The commentator

A 14-minute video summarised the resignation, the essay, the reactions to the essay and the conspiracy readings — then added its own.

Stand with The PrimeTime and the resignation lands three rings out. That is how much interpretation sits between a viewer and the act.

How a ring is assigned. Every edge says what an act references, and carries the sentence that licenses it — shown in the panel whenever you select someone. Distance is then plain arithmetic: hops from the centre. Nothing decides that Sacks is “ring 2”; it falls out of the edges, and re-centring recomputes it.

What this is not. A ring is not importance, and not agreement — Altman agreeing and Sacks attacking sit on the same ring, because both were reading the same document. It is also not the emergence floor: a head of state can sit two hops out.

Standing of the reading. Each edge is an inference from what an act publicly referenced, never from anyone’s stated intent. Some will be wrong. The quote is attached so you can overrule it rather than take it on trust.

Where this reading is thinnest. Select any actor and the panel names the references behind that act, tagged by how well sourced it is.
